About Tamazight

Tamazight (also called Amazigh, or historically "Berber") is the indigenous language family of North Africa, spoken by tens of millions of people across Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mali, Niger, and diaspora communities worldwide. It belongs to the Afro-Asiatic language family and predates the arrival of Arabic in the region by thousands of years.

Amazigh (plural: Imazighen) is how speakers refer to themselves, and it's usually translated as "free people" — the same idea behind the ⵣ symbol in Awal's own logo.

Tamazight isn't a single uniform language — it's a family of related varieties. This app currently focuses on Standard/Central Atlas Tamazight, with some words also shown in Tashelhit (southwestern Morocco) and Tarifit (northern Morocco, the Rif). Tassousiyt, in the app's dialect toggle, refers to the variety of Tashelhit spoken in the Souss region. These dialects share a lot of vocabulary but differ in real, meaningful ways — which is why the app labels a word "general Tamazight" whenever it's showing the standard form instead of a verified dialect one.

Tifinagh is the traditional Amazigh writing system, with roots in ancient Libyco-Berber inscriptions going back over 2,000 years. A modernized version, Neo- Tifinagh, was adopted by Morocco in 2003 as the official script for teaching Tamazight in schools. You'll see it throughout this app alongside the Latin-letter spelling of each word.

The blue, green, and yellow flag colors used throughout this app come from the Amazigh flag, adopted in 1997: blue for the sea and sky, green for the mountains and nature, yellow for the Sahara, and the red ⵣ (yaz) for the Amazigh people themselves.

Awal's word list is a learner's starter set, compiled from published dictionaries and language resources — not a substitute for a native speaker or a linguist. If something looks off, it probably needs a second source; the dataset is a plain JSON file meant to be corrected and grown over time.
